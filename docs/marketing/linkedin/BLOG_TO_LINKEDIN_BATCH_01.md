### Post 1 — Monday (Metric Monday) — Cash flow **[reshare]**

**Format:** text + image (the 9-row monthly table from the cash flow post).
**Comment link:** `cash-flow-positive-rental-properties`

```
"Cash flow positive rental properties" is one of the most searched phrases in real estate investing.

It is also a category error.

Cash flow is not a property attribute. It is a price attribute.

The same 3/2 house:
→ cash flow positive at $270,000
→ a monthly loss at $340,000

Same tenant. Same rent. Same roof.

Three of your five biggest expense lines (property tax, insurance, the mortgage) scale with what you pay. Rent does not. Pay less and three lines fall while income stays fixed.

So the search is backwards. You do not find cash flow positive properties. You price them.

The $325K example in the image: 5.96% cap rate, $55 a month at asking with 20% down. Fine house. Owner-occupant price.

"Off market properties for sale" gets searched thousands of times a month.

### Post 3 — Wednesday — DSCR contrast **[reshare]**

**Format:** text + image (the warning callout "Lender DSCR is not your DSCR" from the DSCR requirements post).
**Comment link:** `dscr-loan-requirements`

```
A DSCR loan can be approved on a property that does not pay you.

Here is how, on the $325K example at typical DSCR terms (75% LTV, 7.25%):

Lender's math
Rent $2,600 ÷ PITIA $2,259 = 1.15
Approvable. Second tier, but approvable.

Your math
NOI after vacancy, maintenance, reserves: $19,370
Debt service: $19,954
DSCR = 0.97

Both numbers are correct. They answer different questions.

The lender is asking: will this loan probably get paid?
You are asking: will this property pay me?

Qualify on the lender's number. Decide on yours.

The full requirements checklist (ratio tiers, LTV, credit, reserves, rent docs, prepay) is in the comments.

#DSCR #RealEstateInvesting #RentalProperty

### Post 4 — Thursday — The $105,000 nobody mentions

**Format:** text only.
**Comment link:** `dscr-loan-requirements`

```
The DSCR ratio is not what stops most first DSCR purchases.

Liquidity is.

On the $325K example at typical program terms:

Down payment (25%): $81,250
Closing costs (~3%): $9,750
Reserves (6 months of PITIA): about $13,600

Cash you need to show: roughly $105,000

Everyone runs the ratio. Almost nobody adds up the third line until the lender asks for a bank statement.

Reserves usually have to be liquid after closing: checking, savings, brokerage, sometimes retirement at a discount. Gift funds and business accounts vary by lender.

If you are planning a DSCR purchase this year, build the liquidity number before you build the deal list. It changes which deal list you should be building.

Checklist in the comments.

#DSCR #RealEstateInvesting
```

### Post 5 — Friday (Creative Finance Friday) — Three questions

**Format:** text + image (the "Three questions for the first call" tip callout).
**Comment link:** `how-to-find-off-market-properties`

```
Three questions before you name a number on an off-market property.

1. Why are you thinking about selling?
2. When do you need this done?
3. Is there a mortgage, and roughly what is owed?

The answers tell you which offer structure has a chance.

No mortgage, no deadline → they want price. Terms will not move them.

A 3% loan and a deadline → they may take terms that cost you far less than the price gap. Seller carry. Subject-to. A lease option.

Off-market does not mean discounted. It means negotiable. And the thing that is negotiable is not always the price.

Which of the three do you ask first?

#CreativeFinance #RealEstateInvesting

## Week 2

### Post 6 — Monday (Metric Monday) — Income Value vs Target Buy

**Format:** text + image (the "$325,000 house, solved for price" example callout).
**Comment link:** `cash-flow-positive-rental-properties`

```
Two prices matter on every rental. Neither is the asking price.

Income Value: the break-even. The most you can pay and still have rent cover every expense and the mortgage with nothing left.

Target Buy: the price that hits the return you actually want.

The $325K example, 20% down, 6%:

Income Value: about $333,000
Target Buy at 8% cash-on-cash: $270,731
Asking: $325,000

So at asking, the property barely covers itself. At Target Buy it pays 8% on your cash, with a DSCR of 1.32.

Deal Gap: 16.7%. That is not "offer 16.7% less." It is the size of the problem you are solving, in price or in terms.

Every listing has these two numbers. Almost no listing shows them.

Full breakdown in the comments.

#RealEstateInvesting #CashFlow #DealGap
```

### Post 8 — Wednesday — Fake cash flow

**Format:** text only.
**Comment link:** `cash-flow-positive-rental-properties`

```
Five ways a listing manufactures cash flow that does not exist.

1. Gross rent counted as income.
$2,600 of rent is $1,614 of NOI on the $325K example. Every skipped line is a future surprise with a plumber attached.

2. The seller's tax bill.
It reflects their purchase price and their exemptions. Yours will reflect yours. $2,100 becomes $3,900 the year after closing.

3. No reserves because the roof is new.
Reserves are for the thing you did not inspect. New roof means the HVAC is next.

4. Appreciation as the reason to accept a loss.
Appreciation is a bonus on a property that already cash flows. As the rescue, it is a bet the market will fix your underwriting.

5. "Cash flow positive" in the remarks.
A claim about the seller's financing and the seller's rent, at a price the seller chose.

Which one has cost you money?

Full post in the comments.

#RealEstateInvesting #RentalProperty #CashFlow
```

**Format:** document carousel, 9 slides. Cover: "Before you call a DSCR lender." Slides 2–8: the seven pre-application items from the post (address/price/units; rent evidence; tax at your price + insurance quote; credit score; liquid funds for down + closing + 6 months PITIA; entity docs; your exit and prepay choice). Slide 9: "Compute the ratio yourself before the lender does. Below 1.20, you already know the negotiation you are walking into."
**Comment link:** `dscr-loan-requirements`

```
Gather these seven things before you call a DSCR lender and the conversation is a rate quote, not a fishing trip.

Swipe through.

The one most people skip is number 7: your exit. DSCR loans commonly carry a 3 to 5 year prepayment penalty. A five-year hold can take the penalty and the lower rate. A BRRRR you plan to refinance in 18 months cannot.

Decide that before the term sheet, not after.

And run the ratio yourself first. If it is below 1.20, the requirement quietly becomes a price negotiation, and you want to walk in knowing that.

Typical program ranges, not rules. The lender's rate sheet is the rule.

### Post 10 — Friday (Creative Finance Friday) — Closing the gap with terms

**Format:** text only. Ends on a question (linkless engagement post per the blueprint's week-8 pattern; the link still goes in the first comment).
**Comment link:** `how-to-make-an-offer-on-an-investment-property` (the offer pillar, since this post bridges to it)

```
When the Deal Gap is too wide for a price cut, you have three other levers.

The $325K example again. Target Buy $270,731. Gap $54,269.

1. Verify rent upward, honestly.
At the top of the estimate range ($2,750) cash flow at asking goes from $55 to $183 a month. Not enough alone. Narrows what terms have to close.

2. Lower the payment with terms.
A seller-carried second at a below-market rate, or taking over an existing low-rate loan. Every $10,000 less of 6% debt removes about $720 a year of payment. On this house, terms can move the number further than any price cut a seller would sign.

3. Change the strategy.
Same property as a house hack or a BRRRR produces a different Target Buy. Sometimes the gap closes without touching price.

(More cash down fixes the sign, not the return. Use it last.)

Which lever do you reach for first when a deal is 15% to 20% off?

#CreativeFinance #RealEstateInvesting #DealGap
```
