## Title

Stop asking "is this a deal?" Ask "how big is the gap?" (worked example, Lake Worth FL)

## Body

Most deal analysis stops at pass/fail: plug in the list price, see if the cash flow or the margin clears your bar. That answer is useful, but it throws away the more useful number, which is how far the listing is from a price that would work.

I call that number the deal gap. It is the difference between what the seller is asking and the most you can pay and still hit your return target.

Worked example, a real address: 1014-16 N J St, Lake Worth, FL. List price $457,100.

Run the numbers at 20% down and back-solve for the purchase price where the deal clears the return target. That comes out at $428,000.

So the deal gap is $428,000 vs $457,100: -6.4%, about $29,000. Negative because the ask is above the target.

Why that beats pass/fail:

1. Pass/fail says "no." The gap says "no, by $29,000." That is a negotiation, not a dead end.
2. It tells you which lever to pull. There are four, plus a blend:
   - Price: the seller comes down.
   - Income: raise the income side so the same price pencils.
   - Terms: change how and when the seller gets paid. Seller financing and subject-to live here.
   - Equity: share or defer equity so less cash goes in on day one.
   - Blend: combine two or more of the above.
3. It makes a normal listing workable. You do not have to mail thousands of owners hoping one is distressed. You can make a structured offer on the house that is already for sale.

Quick habit for your own deals: take any listing, back-solve the price that clears your target, and write down the gap in dollars. Then pick the lever before you write the offer.
